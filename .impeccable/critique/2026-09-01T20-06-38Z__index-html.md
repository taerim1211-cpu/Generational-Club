## Design Specificity Verdict

Authored for this club, not a template -- but shipping with the scaffolding still visible. The strongest evidence: 34 of 38 photos are real session photos (glitter-flower canvases, beaded bracelets, felt hearts), the partnerships are named and correct (Howard County Dept. of Aging, Brighton Gardens, Winter Growth), and the volunteer form is a live, working link. This is a real club's real site.

But specificity drops off exactly where personal effort was needed: 3 of 4 member cards read "Your Name Here," both testimonials are literally the instruction placeholder text, 19 of 34 gallery photos share the caption "Craft session photo," and four separate section subheads still contain editor-facing instructions ("Swap these placeholders...", "Replace these placeholders...") visible to the public.

The detector adds one more specificity-relevant signal the manual review didn't quantify: 10-11 em-dashes across the body copy, flagged by both the CLI scan and the live-page scan as an overuse pattern. On its own this is stylistic, but combined with how uniformly "polished" the written sections are versus how raw the unfinished sections are (literal placeholder text, not attempted-and-bad copy), it's worth asking whether the finished copy was AI-drafted -- which isn't inherently bad, but is worth knowing before deciding how to fill the remaining gaps in the same voice.

## Overall Impression

This is a genuinely well-built single-file site for what it is -- the lightbox alone has better accessibility engineering (focus trap, ARIA, keyboard nav, reduced-motion respect) than most production marketing pages. The problem isn't craft, it's completion: the page is live with its own "fill this in later" notes still showing, at exactly the two places (testimonials, members) most responsible for making a stranger trust a teen-run program with visits to dementia patients. The single biggest opportunity is closing that credibility gap before more traffic arrives.

## What's Working

1. The lightbox is real engineering. Focus trap, role="dialog", aria-modal, Escape/arrow-key handling, focus restoration on close, and prefers-reduced-motion support -- all correct, all present. Both assessments independently confirmed it functions as built.
2. Typography and base accessibility choices are deliberate. Lora/Public Sans pairing at 19px/1.65 line-height, and a genuine :focus-visible outline in a warm accent color rather than relying on the browser default -- someone made real decisions, tuned for an audience that includes older readers.
3. The copy voice consistently respects its subject. Dementia and elder care are discussed with warmth and zero condescension everywhere real copy exists -- a harder needle to thread than it looks.

## Priority Issues

[P0] The testimonials section ships its own instructions as content, positioned right before the CTA.
- Why it matters: Both testimonial cards read "Add a quote from a resident, volunteer, or facility staff member here." This sits at the exact peak-end position -- right before "Apply to Volunteer" -- where trust should be highest.
- Fix: Populate with 1-2 real quotes, or remove the section entirely until they exist.
- Suggested command: /impeccable clarify

[P1] The header/CTA-band cyan (#bbf1f1) fails contrast AND clashes tonally -- confirmed two independent ways.
- Why it matters: The manual review flagged this cyan as louder/more saturated than the rest of the otherwise warm, muted palette. The detector then measured an actual WCAG failure inside that same header: the teal tagline text on this cyan background is 3.9:1, below the 4.5:1 AA minimum.
- Fix: Darken/desaturate the header cyan to meet 4.5:1 against the tagline text, or move the tagline to a darker passing color; consider pulling the header background toward the existing --bg-alt/--primary family.
- Suggested command: /impeccable colorize

[P1] 19 of 34 gallery photos share the generic caption "Craft session photo," with no grouping or progressive disclosure.
- Why it matters: Fails the cognitive-load checklist's "minimal choices" and "progressive disclosure" checks -- a flat 34-item grid with no sectioning reads as an inventory dump, and screen-reader users get no real information from two-thirds of the section.
- Fix: Write specific alt text for craft-0 through craft-18; consider grouping into 2-3 labeled clusters or a "see more" expansion.
- Suggested command: /impeccable clarify

[P1] 3 of 4 member cards are unfilled "Your Name Here" placeholders.
- Why it matters: This section exists to humanize the club for skeptical visitors deciding whether to trust it with dementia-care visits. Three-quarters placeholder does the opposite of its job.
- Fix: Fill in real names/roles, or shrink the grid to only the members actually populated.
- Suggested command: /impeccable clarify

[P2] Editor-facing scaffolding copy is shipped live in section subheads.
- Why it matters: "Swap these placeholders for your own club photos as you take them" (Photos) and "Replace these placeholders with your own club officers and members" (Members) are maintainer instructions visible to every visitor.
- Fix: Remove this copy from both section leads regardless of whether the underlying content gaps are filled first.
- Suggested command: /impeccable clarify

## Minor Observations

- Detector flagged the header's dashed bottom border and the quote-cards' border-left accent as a "side-tab" pattern, a generic decorative tell it associates with AI-generated design. The manual reviewer read the same header border positively, as a tactile "craft/scrapbook" touch. Worth deciding deliberately rather than by default.
- A body paragraph runs ~93 characters per line (aiming for <80) at wide viewports.
- Footer's "Refer a Facility" link points to #involved -- the volunteer-apply block, not anything about referring a facility.
- The .trust strip says "Fully student-run" directly under a header crediting an adult county department -- not contradictory, but a natural question a skeptical parent might want answered nearby.
- CLI detector's broken-image flag on the lightbox's empty-src img (line 799) is a confirmed false positive -- it's the lightbox target image, populated by JS only when opened.
