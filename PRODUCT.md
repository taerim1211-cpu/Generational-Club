# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Two audiences, weighted equally:

1. **Prospective teen volunteers** — Marriotts Ridge High School students browsing club options, deciding whether to sign up. Job: quickly understand what the club does, that no art experience is needed, and how to join.
2. **Adults evaluating trust** — parents of prospective volunteers, senior-living facility staff, and Howard County Department of Aging partner contacts. Job: assess whether visits are safe, organized, and genuinely beneficial to residents before endorsing or allowing participation.

## Product Purpose

The Generational Club is a student-run volunteer club at Marriotts Ridge High School. Members make monthly visits to local senior living facilities to lead craft and painting activities for residents living with dementia — combating isolation and helping maintain fine motor skills — in partnership with the Howard County Department of Aging. Success means students sign up via the volunteer form, and adults viewing the site trust the program enough to allow or recommend participation.

## Positioning

Not a generic "volunteer club" — a specific, institutionally-backed pipeline: teen-led, free, no art experience required, coordinated directly with the Howard County Department of Aging and named senior living facilities, with dementia-aware training built into onboarding.

## Operating Context

- Monthly visits to named senior living communities: Brighton Gardens of Columbia, Winter Growth Assisted Living Center.
- Dementia-aware training co-led with the Howard County Department of Aging, part of the 4-step onboarding: Apply → Train → Get Matched → Start Visiting.
- Real craft activities members lead: beaded jewelry, painted drawstring bags, canvas painting, memory collages, felt crafts, seasonal ornaments.
- Club also runs bake-sale fundraisers, a portion of proceeds benefiting the Alzheimer's Association.

## Product Principles

1. Prove the program is real through the club's own photographic evidence, never stock imagery or invented claims.
2. Make the ask (become a volunteer) feel low-friction and welcoming to a first-timer with zero art experience.
3. Earn adult trust through visible institutional backing (the county partnership, named facilities, dementia-aware training) rather than generic reassurance copy.
4. Treat the dementia/elder-care subject with warmth and dignity — never twee, never clinical.
5. Keep the craft/handmade energy authentic to what teens actually make rather than importing generic "volunteering" stock visuals.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Audience includes older readers (parents, facility staff), so legible type sizes matter. Prior work established focus-visible states, a keyboard-accessible lightbox, and baseline color contrast; the redesign should preserve or improve on this rather than regress it.
